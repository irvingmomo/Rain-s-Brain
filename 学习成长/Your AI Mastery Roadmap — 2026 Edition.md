## First, Know What You Are Building Toward

You are not training to become an AI engineer or researcher. You are building **AI fluency** — the ability to understand, evaluate, and leverage AI tools intelligently in your professional and personal life. Think of it like learning to drive well, not learning to build a car engine.

## PHASE 1 — Foundations (Months 1–3)

### You SHOULD understand:

**Core concepts — learn these in plain English first:**

- What is a neural network (and why it matters)
- What is machine learning vs. deep learning vs. AI (the hierarchy)
- What is a Large Language Model (LLM) — how tokenisation, training, and inference work at a conceptual level
- What is "training data" and why it shapes AI behaviour
- What are embeddings, vectors, and why AI "understands" language
- What is prompt engineering — and why it is a real skill, not a trick
- What are hallucinations, and why they happen structurally (not randomly)
- What is Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) — this explains why Claude, GPT, and Gemini behave the way they do

**You SHOULD NOT:**

- ❌ Learn Python just to understand AI concepts (useful later, not now)
- ❌ Study linear algebra or calculus deeply — a conceptual grasp is enough
- ❌ Touch older AI paradigms: Lisp, expert systems, symbolic AI, Prolog — historically interesting, practically irrelevant for your goals
- ❌ Learn to build or fine-tune models — that is an AI engineer's job

**Best resources for Phase 1:**

- 📺 **3Blue1Brown** — "Neural Networks" series on YouTube (visual, no maths required)
- 📺 **Andrej Karpathy** — "Intro to Large Language Models" (1 hour, conceptually gold)
- 📖 **"AI Basics for Non-Programmers"** — short, practical
- 📖 **"The Alignment Problem"** by Brian Christian — explains how AI learns values (reads like a novel)
- 🎧 **Lex Fridman Podcast** — pick episodes with Sam Altman, Yann LeCun, Geoffrey Hinton

## PHASE 2 — Tools & Daily Practice (Months 2–8, overlaps with Phase 1)

_Start this early — do not wait until you "understand enough." Practice and theory reinforce each other._

### You SHOULD master these tools:

**Tier 1 — Use every single day:**

|Tool|Why|
|---|---|
|**Claude (Anthropic)**|Best for reasoning, long documents, nuanced work|
|**ChatGPT (OpenAI)**|Widest ecosystem, most integrations, great for comparison|
|**Gemini (Google)**|Best for Google Workspace integration, multimodal tasks|
|**Perplexity AI**|AI-powered research and web search — replace Google for most queries|
|**NotebookLM**|You already use this — deepen it for knowledge management|

**Tier 2 — Learn and use regularly:**

|Tool|Why|
|---|---|
|**Cursor / GitHub Copilot**|Even as a non-developer, you will automate Excel, scripts, and data tasks|
|**Midjourney / DALL-E**|Visual AI literacy — understand generative image models|
|**ElevenLabs**|Voice/audio AI — important for future of communication|
|**Make (Integromat) or Zapier with AI**|Automate workflows without code|
|**Microsoft Copilot**|Embedded in Office 365 — directly relevant to your daily work|

**Tier 3 — Be aware, experiment occasionally:**

- Grok (xAI), Mistral, DeepSeek (understand the competitive landscape)
- Sora / Runway (video AI — early stage but moving fast)
- Claude Code / Cursor Agents (agentic AI — the near future of automation)

### You SHOULD NOT:

- ❌ Try to master every tool — you will drown. Tier 1 is your daily habit
- ❌ Treat tools as static — every model updates quarterly. Build the habit of reading release notes
- ❌ Ignore enterprise AI tools relevant to your industry (supply chain AI, procurement analytics)
- ❌ Skip comparing tools on the same task — this is how you build real judgment

### Practice method — the "Daily Reps" habit:

> Every day, use at least one AI tool for a real work task. Never use Google when you can ask Perplexity. Never write a first draft alone when Claude can start it. Never manually summarise a document when NotebookLM exists.

## PHASE 3 — History & Future / AGI Literacy (Months 6–12)

_Do this last — context lands better once you have hands-on experience._

### The AI Timeline You SHOULD know:

|Era|Key Event|Why It Matters|
|---|---|---|
|1950s|Turing Test, Dartmouth Conference|Origin of the field|
|1980s–90s|Expert systems, AI winter|Why rule-based AI failed|
|2012|AlexNet / ImageNet breakthrough|Deep learning revolution begins|
|2017|"Attention Is All You Need" paper|Transformers invented — everything modern is built on this|
|2020|GPT-3|Public inflection point|
|2022|ChatGPT launch|Mass adoption begins|
|2024–25|Reasoning models, agents, multimodal|Where we are now|
|2026+|Agentic AI, physical AI, AGI debate|Where you are heading|

### AGI — You SHOULD understand:

- What AGI actually means (general vs. narrow intelligence)
- The difference between **AGI** and **superintelligence** — they are not the same
- The main AGI frameworks: OpenAI's 5-level scale, DeepMind's approach, Anthropic's Constitutional AI
- Why **alignment** matters — this is not science fiction, it is an active engineering problem
- Key thinkers: Geoffrey Hinton, Yoshua Bengio (worried), Yann LeCun (skeptical), Sam Altman (optimistic), Demis Hassabis (pragmatic)

### You SHOULD NOT:

- ❌ Get caught in AGI doomsday rabbit holes — understand the debate, don't obsess
- ❌ Treat any single prediction as fact — nobody knows the timeline
- ❌ Ignore AGI either — it will restructure your industry within your career horizon

**Best resources for Phase 3:**

- 📖 **"The Coming Wave"** by Mustafa Suleyman — written by a founder, readable, balanced
- 📖 **"Superintelligence"** by Nick Bostrom — dense but foundational to the AGI debate
- 📺 **Geoffrey Hinton's Nobel Prize lecture** (2024) — a pioneer's warning, 45 minutes
- 📺 **Andrej Karpathy's talks** on AI software 2.0

## Standing Rules — All Year

✅ **Follow these newsletters:** The Batch (Andrew Ng), TLDR AI, Stratechery, Ars Technica AI section ✅ **Set a 30-minute weekly review** — what changed in AI this week? ✅ **Build a NotebookLM "AI knowledge base"** — you already know the tool ✅ **Document your own AI usage** — what worked, what failed, what surprised you ✅ **Talk to Claude about AI** — ask it to explain concepts, challenge your understanding, quiz you

❌ **Do not study:** PyTorch, TensorFlow, model architecture code, AI safety mathematics, academic papers (read summaries instead) ❌ **Do not benchmark tools for fun** — always benchmark for a real task you actually need done ❌ **Do not chase every new model release** — note it, test it briefly, move on

## The Single Most Important Advice

> **Use AI to learn AI.**

Every concept you do not understand — ask Claude to explain it three different ways. Every tool you are curious about — ask ChatGPT what it is good for. Every AGI article that confuses you — paste it into NotebookLM and interrogate it. You already have the best teacher in history in your browser. The students who learn fastest are the ones who treat the AI itself as the textbook.

By end of 2026, you will not be an AI engineer — but you will be something rarer and more valuable: a domain expert who understands AI deeply enough to direct it, question it, and make it work for you.
