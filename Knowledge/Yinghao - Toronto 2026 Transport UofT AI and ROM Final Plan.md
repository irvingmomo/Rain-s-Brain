# 2. Getting inside / credentials

## Normal arrival point

Bahen Centre for Information Technology
40 St George St

Department of Computer Science main reception:
- **Room BA4283, 4th floor**

Do **not** begin by wandering directly to 7th/8th-floor labs. Treat the visit as a hosted academic visit:
1. enter through the St. George Street entrance during weekday public hours
2. go to the agreed professor/lab meeting point or DCS reception
3. have the host escort you into any restricted lab/office area

## Lab access

CS teaching labs are TCard-controlled. Public access to the Bahen building does **not** mean public access to labs, offices or meeting rooms.

## Guest identity / Wi-Fi options

### Best if a professor is formally hosting the visit
Ask the professor / lab manager / business officer to sponsor a **Short-Term Guest UTORid** (appropriate for a visit of 5 days or less).

Important distinction:
- short-term Guest UTORid = digital university guest credential
- it does **not automatically guarantee every lab/door permission**
- the host still needs to arrange any required physical access

### Simplest if only internet is needed
Use **eduroam Visitor Access (eVA)**.

Process:
1. a U of T staff/faculty member accesses the internal eVA portal
2. they give you the current SMS keyword/number, or create guest credentials
3. you receive a temporary username/password by SMS
4. connect to the `eduroam` network
5. daily credentials normally need renewal each day unless the sponsor creates a multi-day guest credential

If your own university/institution already participates in eduroam, test your home-institution eduroam credentials before leaving Australia.

## “Visitor card” clarification

There is no simple tourist badge that automatically opens Bahen labs.

Also do not confuse:
- **TCard** = U of T identity/access card for eligible users
- **Guest TCard / print card** sold by the library = payment card for printing/copying; it is not a Bahen door-access card

# 3. Where to work all day

## Primary workspace: Bahen public study areas — on weekdays

Bahen has multiple informal study areas. Use them between meetings when you want to remain physically inside the CS/engineering environment.

Best use:
- email / writing / coding between meetings
- short calls with headphones
- waiting for professors
- staying close to labs

Limitations:
- first-come, first-served
- can be noisy
- power availability varies by seat
- not a guaranteed private office

## Do not plan on CS teaching labs as a visitor workspace

They are card-controlled and primarily for authorized CS students.

## Meeting rooms

Assume **host-booked only**. Ask the professor/lab manager to reserve a room if a meeting needs privacy, screen sharing or whiteboard use. Do not arrive expecting to self-book a department room as an outside visitor.

## Best quiet fallback: Robarts Library / Robarts Common

Robarts is better than a café for several hours of serious laptop work because the Common has large numbers of study seats and power outlets.

However, Robarts entry for an outside visitor is controlled:
- request a free one-day research visitor pass in advance
- the request should state a genuine research purpose and identify specific print material you want to consult
- bring the approval email and government photo ID
- up to three one-day visitor passes per month may be approved
- bookable study rooms are for current U of T users; as a visitor use first-come, non-bookable seating

Important: guest Wi-Fi does not automatically provide access to licensed U of T e-journals/databases.

## Engineering & Computer Science Library

This sounds ideal, but it is currently marked **U of T access**, not general public access. Do not build the visit around entering it unless your host/guest status gives you legitimate access.

## Printing — exact visitor workflow

At U of T Libraries:
- buy a **Guest TCard / print-copy card** at Robarts
- current cost: CAD 5, including CAD 4 print/copy credit and CAD 1 card fee
- black-and-white letter printing starts around CAD 0.15/side on B&W printers
- colour letter printing is around CAD 1/page
- scanning is free at most libraries

Visitor limitation:
- library desktop computer printing is restricted to current students/faculty/staff
- as a visitor, plan to print **wirelessly from your own laptop** where supported, using the guest print card

Bring a USB stick for scanning.
