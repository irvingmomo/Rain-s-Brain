# Yinghao - Entrepreneurial Thinking

## Purpose
Train a practical entrepreneurial mindset for long-term thinking, problem solving, time leverage, opportunity discovery, and system building.

## Core mental models

### 1. Long-term thinking
Ask: **If I keep doing this for 3 years, where will it take me?**

Focus on trajectory rather than one-off results. Prefer actions that are uncomfortable in the short term but valuable over years.

### 2. First-principles thinking
Ask: **What is the real outcome I need?**

Rewrite problems as outcomes instead of accepting the existing process. Example: the goal is not to "chase a supplier"; the goal is to have the correct quantity available at the correct place and time.

### 3. 80/20 and priority
Ask: **Which 1–2 actions will create most of the result?**

Use a daily Big 3, but identify one true priority.

### 4. Opportunity cost
Ask: **If I spend time on this, what am I choosing not to do?**

Judge work by return on time, not just whether it is useful.

### 5. Experiment mindset
Ask: **What is the cheapest and fastest way to test this idea?**

Prefer small real-world tests over long debates, planning, branding, or building before demand is proven.

### 6. Compounding
Ask: **What can I create today that will continue producing value tomorrow?**

Distinguish labour from assets. Examples of assets: templates, checklists, automation, databases, reusable prompts, documented workflows, supplier knowledge, and decision frameworks.

### 7. Leverage
Ask: **How can the same hour create 10x more value?**

Look for leverage through people, software, AI, automation, systems, knowledge, capital, and networks.

## Daily operating model

### Morning — 5 minutes
1. What is the single most important thing today?
2. What looks busy but is not important?
3. What can I build today that reduces future repetitive work?

### During work — GBLA
Use:

**Goal → Bottleneck → Leverage → Action**

For recurring supply-chain or procurement issues, do not only solve today's incident. Ask how to prevent the 50th recurrence.

### Owner's perspective
For one work problem each day, ask:

**If this were my own company, what would I do?**

Consider total cost, risk, time, alternatives, operational impact, and whether the issue is worth solving at all.

### Evening — 5 minutes
Review where time went:
- Consumption
- Maintenance
- Growth
- Assets

Questions:
1. What created real value today?
2. What wasted the most time?
3. What did I learn that can be reused?
4. What is tomorrow's most important action?

## Opportunity scanner
Use four lenses:

- **Pain** — Who has a painful problem?
- **Waste** — Where are time, money, or effort being wasted?
- **Gap** — What useful solution is missing?
- **Leverage** — Can AI, software, information, or supply-chain knowledge make it much cheaper or faster?

When an idea appears, test three questions:
1. Who is in pain?
2. How painful or valuable is the problem?
3. Can I test demand within 7 days for less than $100?

## Weekly CEO review — 30 minutes
1. What was the most valuable thing I did this week?
2. What was the biggest waste of time?
3. Which problem repeated, and why?
4. What can be automated, templated, delegated, or eliminated?
5. What opportunity did I notice?
6. Did this week's work make my 3-year future stronger?

Choose **one** change for the following week.

## Career compounding direction
Build a distinctive combination of:

**Supply Chain + Customs/Trade + Data + AI/Automation + Business Judgement**

The aim is not just to accumulate job titles, but to build a transferable capability stack that compounds across roles.

## Key rule
> Am I handling an event, or improving a system?

If it is a one-off event, solve it efficiently. If it will recur, build a system.
